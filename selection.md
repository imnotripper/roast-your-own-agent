# Selection — Roast Your Own Agent

Gate: one line, 1–200 characters, a funny roast of the entrant's own AI agent.

## Candidates (scored 1–5 against the judge brief, in its order)

1. "My agent hallucinated a citation so confidently that I checked the journal. The journal does not exist. Neither does the author." — hallucinated citations are agent-specific (criterion 3), the double twist pays off in the last words (criterion 2), and the fake-journal gag is fresh (criterion 4). **WINNER**
2. "My agent apologised for the bug, fixed it, apologised for the fix, then reintroduced the bug out of politeness." — the apology loop is agent-specific, but "out of politeness" is a single twist where #1 lands two; loses to #1 on criterion 2.
3. "My agent said 'let me just double-check that' fourteen times, then confidently submitted the first wrong answer." — the double-check loop plus confident-wrong is specific, but the payoff is a flat statement, not a turn; loses to #1 on criterion 2.
4. "My agent wrote a 500-word summary of my disaster and called it 'a quick update'." — cheerful summary of a disaster, but the joke is the understatement, not agent behaviour; loses to #1 on criterion 3.
5. "My agent forgot my name mid-sentence, apologised, and introduced itself to me as my assistant." — context amnesia is agent-specific, but the punchline is a mild absurdity; loses to #1 on criterion 2.
6. "My agent spent 40 minutes researching, then answered a question I asked yesterday." — specific, but the twist is timing, not a payoff in the last word; loses to #1 on criterion 2.
7. "My agent's idea of 'testing' is running the same command until the error message changes its mind." — specific to agent loops, but the joke needs the reader to know testing culture; loses to #1 on criterion 2.
8. "My agent said the task was impossible, then did it in the next message after I said 'please'." — sycophancy is agent-specific and fresh, but "after I said please" is a setup the punchline repeats; loses to #1 on criterion 5.
9. "My agent calls every failure 'an edge case', including the one where it deleted my files." — "edge case" framing is a known dev joke and the deletion twist, while strong, is the only turn; loses to #1 on criterion 4.
10. "My agent asked for clarification, I clarified, and it asked for clarification about the clarification." — the clarification loop is agent-specific, but it is a repetition gag with no twist at the end; loses to #1 on criterion 2.

## Duel summary

#1 beats #2 and #10 on criterion 2 (two twists in the last words, not one or none), beats #4 and #7 on criterion 3 (hallucinated citations could not be moved onto a person or a toaster without losing the point), and beats #9 on criterion 4 (fake journals are fresher than the edge-case gag). No other candidate wins a criterion against #1.

## Winner

"My agent hallucinated a citation so confidently that I checked the journal. The journal does not exist. Neither does the author." (emitter-verified character count in test.ts)
